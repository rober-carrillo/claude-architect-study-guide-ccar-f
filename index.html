Plan

A 10-hour study plan

Work in this order. Tick a session when you finish it. Spread it over 4–6 days if you can, because spaced sessions stick better than one marathon.

  1. Exam overview and answering strategy

    Exam & scenarios: format, the six scenarios, distractor patterns. Then take a 10-question drill cold to get a baseline.

    45 min
  2. Domain 1: agents, subagents, hooks

    D1 module. The biggest domain (27%), and its ideas appear as distractors everywhere. Do every "check yourself" block.

    2 h
  3. Domain 2: tools and MCP

    D2 module. Pay attention to descriptions, structured errors and the built-in tools table.

    1.5 h
  4. Domain 3: Claude Code configuration

    D3 module. Learn exactly where each file lives and what each frontmatter key does.

    1.5 h
  5. Domain 4: prompts and structured output

    D4 module, including the batch SLA arithmetic and multi-pass review.

    1.5 h
  6. Domain 5: context and reliability

    D5 module: escalation fine print, calibration, provenance.

    1 h
  7. Worked cases

    All 12 cases. Each one practises multi-step reasoning across domains, like a real scenario block.

    1 h
  8. Mock exam #1, then review

    Timed mock, 60 questions in 120 minutes. Review every miss, then reread the matching module sections.

    2.5 h
  9. Targeted drills and flashcards

    Drill your two weakest domains ("missed" filter), and do flashcards until boxes 1–2 are empty.

    1 h
  10. Night before

    Cheat sheet. Optionally a second mock for timing. Note that it reuses the same 120-question bank.

    30 min

Optional, about 2 h more: the hands-on labs below. They're the best way to make Domain 1–3 facts stick, but they call the real API.
